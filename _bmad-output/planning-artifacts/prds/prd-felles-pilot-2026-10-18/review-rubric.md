# PRD Quality Review — FELLES

## Overall verdict
Adequate and usable as a delta layer on `docs/requirements.md`. The decision log (DEC-01..19), the per-requirement owner slices, and the delivery-status, evidence and gate rules (PIL-01..11) are concrete and let UX, architecture and story workflows start. The risks are these. The PRD never says how the pilot is judged a success beyond "traceability exists". One cross-reference (§9.1) points at a section that does not exist. A few terms (Phase B, S00–S07) are used without definition in the PRD itself. The gate that makes a human accept each story is in tension with the parallelism the pilot wants to test, and the PRD does not say so.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The decisions are stated as decisions, in a table with IDs, rationale and a "Material" flag that tells architecture what it must honour. DEC-13 openly records that the spec's §14 "implied browser proof earlier than the slice plan allows". DEC-19 settles the question of authority. The four open questions are real: OQ-1 on whether sprint-status supports the six-state vocabulary, OQ-2 on the Orchestrator input format, OQ-3 on metrics, and OQ-4 on the GitHub remote. Each says what it blocks or who owns it.

Trade-offs are mostly one-line rationales ("Spec silent", "Removes ambiguity"). They name what was chosen, not what was given up. DEC-05 leaves expired requests as `pending` in the database, so the data is dirty and every consumer must derive "utløpt". DEC-03 trades usefulness for privacy by hiding purpose. DEC-06 makes archiving irreversible. None of these cost lines are written down. The tension between PIL-02 ("accepted: a human (Jo) decided so") with ORK-02 (dependents count a slice as done only after acceptance) and the parallel plan DEC-18 (S01 ∥ S02, then S03) is not surfaced. The human becomes the critical path.

### Findings
- **medium** Human-acceptance gate vs parallelism not acknowledged (§7 PIL-02, PIL-06; §9 DEC-18) — If acceptance by Jo gates dependents, S03 cannot start until Jo has accepted both S01 and S02, which reduces the benefit of the parallel experiment. Whether that is intended is not stated. *Fix:* Add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` or a DEC entry. State whether dependents may start on `reviewed` and only count as done on `accepted`, and record the cost either way.
- **low** Decision rationales omit cost (§9, DEC-03, DEC-05, DEC-06) — *Fix:* Add a short "gives up" clause to the Material decisions. Architecture will need it when it hits the derived-"utløpt" complexity.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is no persona theater. There are three jobs-to-be-done, and the Pilot owner is a real stakeholder who drives §7–8. NFRs are not boilerplate. They carry product-specific rules: DST changeover dates in DEC-09, BEGIN IMMEDIATE atomicity in DEC-12, a named cookie and Origin check in NFR-04, and `npm run verify` on Windows and macOS in PIL-09. The Vision names the two-fold purpose (a finished product and a reusable workflow test). That is specific enough that it could not be swapped into another PRD. There is no innovation section, which is appropriate.

### Findings
- **low** Soft NFR wording carried over (§4.6, "WCAG 2.2 AA where practical", NFR-06 "Responsive local lookups"; §8 PIL-11 "no known severe defects") — The PRD points to the spec for thresholds, and I could not verify those from the PRD. "Severe" is not defined anywhere in the PRD. *Fix:* Define "severe" in PIL-11, for example as a failed MUST acceptance scenario or a data-loss or security defect. Quote the NFR-06 threshold inline if the spec has one.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and unusual: the app is the test subject, and the pilot succeeds only if each requirement can be traced to builder, change, test and human acceptance (§1). PIL-01..08 follow from it, and the MVP is a problem-solving scope. What is missing is success metrics. PIL-07 specifies what is recorded (time, runs, interventions, retries, review findings, cost-or-`unknown`), but not what result would count as "trustworthy enough to reuse" (§2.1, Pilot owner JTBD). There are no counter-metrics, for example a limit on human interventions per story, or an explicit check that process overhead did not swamp delivery.

### Findings
- **high** No success criterion for the pilot thesis (§1, §2.1, §7 PIL-07) — "Judge whether this workflow is trustworthy enough" has no stated threshold. PIL-07 collects data but does not interpret it, so the pilot can end without a verdict. *Fix:* Add 3–5 pilot success measures with thresholds or at least decision rules, for example "every accepted MUST has a complete PIL-08 chain", "zero stories reached `accepted` without recorded `tested` evidence", "human interventions per story trend". Add one or two counter-metrics, for example total time spent on process vs on code.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Done-ness is deliberately delegated: "Acceptance text for every ID is in `docs/requirements.md` §3 and is authoritative" (§4), plus E2E-01..10 as mandatory evidence (§4.7). That is acceptable here, and the PRD adds testable boundaries where the spec was silent: DEC-04 window and ranking, DEC-05 error `BOOKING_STARTED`, DEC-08 boundary rules, DEC-09 DST dates, DEC-14 limits, and DEC-16 status codes. PIL-01 requires "at least one named automated test" per MUST. The status model PIL-02 gives each state an observable evidence condition. The §4 tables, however, are capability labels (for example "Availability display and clear responses; server is truth", BOK-07). A story author must open the Norwegian spec for each one, so the PRD alone is not enough for stories. That is by design but makes the spec a hard dependency.

### Findings
- **medium** Evidence mapping to E2E scenarios is partial (§4.7) — Only Phase B is mapped (E2E-01..03, -07 as API-level). The sentence "E2E-04…06, -08, -09 land with S05/S02/S05" is cryptic: it pairs six numbers with three slices without saying which maps to which. S06 (dashboard/activity) and VIS-03..06 have no named E2E scenario. *Fix:* Replace with a small table of E2E-xx, slice, and evidence type (API test, Playwright), and say which scenario covers VIS-03..05.
- **low** Spec cross-references are opaque to anyone without the spec (§7 PIL-05 "spec §11", PIL-06 "spec §12 rule 5", PIL-02 "ORK-02", DEC-13 "spec §14") — These are legitimate, but ORK-02 is used in the status rule without explanation. *Fix:* Add a half-sentence for each in place, for example "(ORK-02: dependents wait for acceptance)".

## Scope honesty — adequate
§5 Non-Goals is specific and adds v1 exclusions beyond the spec (registration, password change, un-archiving, editing a booking's time). §6 says SHOULD items are last-in-slice and listed as residual work if undelivered. DEC-19 and the §0 statement that no change in the Requirement Change Register is approved yet make de-scoping explicit. Open-item density is low: four open questions, and none blocks product behaviour. All four concern the pilot tooling.

### Findings
- **medium** Dangling Requirement Change Register (§0, §9 DEC-19) — Both refer to "§9.1", and the PRD has no §9.1. A reader cannot tell whether the register is empty or missing. *Fix:* Add a §9.1 with a one-line "No changes proposed or approved" row, or remove the reference.
- **medium** Assumptions are not tagged where they live (§11, §9) — DEC-03..08, -11, -14, -15 are listed as inferred and "confirmed by blanket approval", but the Decision Log rows do not carry an `[ASSUMPTION]` marker. A downstream reader scanning DEC rows cannot tell which defaults are unconfirmed from the table. Blanket approval is also weak evidence for security-relevant defaults (DEC-11 session lifetime, hashing). *Fix:* Add an "Assumed" column or inline tag to the table and drop the "Revisit… at this review" line once reviewed.
- **low** Unclear priority wording (§9 DEC-11) — "basic login throttling (in-memory) is SHOULD-level", but it appears in neither §4 nor §6's SHOULD list (RES-05, BOK-08, VIS-06, NFR-06). *Fix:* Add it to the §6 residual-work list or to AUT-01.

## Downstream usability — adequate
IDs are contiguous: PIL-01..11, DEC-01..19, OQ-1..4. UJ-1..4 each have a named protagonist (Ingrid, Magnus) with the role inline, and they map to E2E scenarios. The Glossary is short and used consistently: Blocking status, Expired request, Approval Requirement and Activity Event all appear identically in the DECs. Two gaps hurt downstream workflows. The slice names S00–S07 and "Phase B" are central (every Owner column, DEC-13, DEC-18, §4.7) but never defined in the PRD. Only S01/S02/S03/S07 and the foundation role of S00 can be inferred. The UJs cover happy paths and one conflict. There is no journey for Admin cancelling another user's booking (DEC-07), the "utløpt" experience (DEC-05), or an archive refusal (DEC-06). UX will need those for the states and error screens.

### Findings
- **high** Slices and phases undefined in the PRD (§4 Owner columns, §4.7, §9 DEC-13/18) — The `Owner` column and the sequencing are the PRD's main addition, yet S00..S07 and "Phase B" are defined only in the spec's Part B. The §7 text calls Part B "a starting plan, adopted in §7–8", but §7–8 do not list the slices. *Fix:* Add a compact slice table (ID, name, one-line scope, depends-on) to §6 or §7. Add "Phase B = S00–S03" if that is what it means.
- **medium** UJ coverage misses the decision-heavy edge flows (§2.3) — Add UJ-5 (Admin cancels another's booking, with reason, per DEC-07). Add UJ-6 (member sees an expired request, "utløpt"). Add a brief archive-refusal variant in UJ-3. *Fix:* Add these or note in §2.3 that UX derives them from DEC-05/06/07.
- **low** Glossary gaps (§3) — Terms used in the PRD but not defined: "Slice", "Task card", "Phase B", "Pilot owner" (the only entry in §2.1 that is not a Member/Administrator role), and UI labels such as "Mine bookinger" (appears in UJ-1 and VIS-02 in Norwegian). *Fix:* Add them, with the Norwegian UI label in parentheses when it is a fixed label.
- **low** Addendum is helpful and flagged non-binding; the "Migration numbering needs a collision rule" is an unresolved item that should be mirrored as an architecture to-do. *Fix:* Add it to §10 or to DEC-18.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a brownfield-style delta PRD over an authoritative spec, paired with a process-experiment layer. The shape fits. UJs are a few, derived from E2E scenarios and marked "names are illustrative", which is proportionate for a small multi-role app with an Admin approval flow. A requirements table with owner slices and a separate pilot-requirements section (PIL-xx) clearly distinguish product behaviour from process rules (§7 "process requirements for the experiment, not product features"). The PRD keeps the spec's IDs (rejected FR-N renumbering in the addendum), so traceability is preserved. It is neither over-formalized nor thin for the stakes.

- **Cross-references:** §9.1 does not exist (§0, DEC-19). ORK-02, spec §3, §4, §6, §7, §9, §10, §11, §12, §14 and Part A/B were not verified because `docs/requirements.md` was out of scope for this review. "Spec §10" is cited as both the E2E list (§4.7) and the definition of done (PIL-11). Confirm both are right.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** §11 lists nine DEC items, but there are no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags to round-trip. DEC-03..08, -11, -14 and -15 match the Index. DEC-01/02 are marked "(Jo)" and DEC-09, -10, -12, -13, -16..19 are neither marked nor indexed, so their status (assumed or confirmed) is unstated.
- **Glossary drift:** "Booking" and "Resource" are capitalized consistently. "Member"/"Administrator" are consistent, though UJ-3 uses "Administrator Sofie" and §4 uses "Admin" (RES-03 "Admin create/edit", VIS-04 "Admin 7-day panel"). "Expired request" is used as defined. `confirmed`/`pending` are code-formatted consistently. The shorthand "Admin" is minor.
- **Minor consistency:** DEC-04 counts "non-expired pending" and DEC-05 excludes expired from "queue and counts", which agree. DEC-06 archive guard on "future pending/confirmed" is consistent with expired requests being in the past.
- **Frontmatter:** `status: draft`, with no `inputs` or `author` fields; fine for the stakes.
